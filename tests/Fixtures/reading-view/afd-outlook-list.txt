.DISCUSSION...
As of 139 AM EST Thursday...

KEY MESSAGE 1: A strong low pressure system will track northward from 
interior North Carolina into western Pennsylvania this afternoon. 
The overall track of the low has not changed in recent model runs 
with the only difference being a 2-3 hour delay in the onset of 
precipitation across our forecast area. The track is favorable for 
strong warm air advection in the low and mid-levels which will be 
supportive of widespread rainfall across the region tonight into 
Friday morning. Given the initial cold currently overhead, we expect 
this warm air to overrun the cold air at the summits and some valley 
locations leading to a period of freezing rain this evening and 
overnight tonight. Freezing rain totals generally have stayed the 
same across the high terrain and sheltered valleys but we have 
increased icing totals across the St. Lawrence Valley significantly. 
Earlier in the day, there was some question about how cold the 
northeasterly winds would be throughout the valley with the high 
pressure system to the north. It's becoming increasingly clear that 
the St. Lawrence Valley will be able to advect in air in the 28-31 
degree range through much of the event, leading to freezing rain 
accumulation up to a quarter of an inch.

With this load of icing and winds gusting up to 30 mph, it's 
possible that we see a few localized power outages due to the weight 
of the ice and increased stress from the winds. Eventually, sometime 
Friday morning, temperatures will warm above freezing with all 
precipitation changing to rain. Once a cold front comes through 
later in the day on Friday, we will see a mix of mountain snow 
showers and valley rain showers continue off and on in the 
northwesterly flow through Saturday.

KEY MESSAGE 2: Models continue to show a strong low level jet moving 
overhead this evening with 900 mb winds approaching 60 knots. This 
will lead to strong winds mid-mountain and summit level with some 
gap winds impacting Rutland County. Right now, it seems like 35-45 
mph winds will be possible in Rutland County tonight with 45 to 60 
mph possible as you get closer to summit level. Elsewhere, winds 
don't appear to be nearly as strong with max wind gusts in the 15 to 
25 mph range. As noted earlier, the channeled northeasterly winds in 
the St. Lawrence Valley could top 30 mph which when combined with 
icing could create some utility outages. Across southern Vermont, we 
expect isolated outages as a result of the stronger winds but 
outages will likely be limited to Rutland and Bennington Counties. 
The one fly in the ointment, will be how quickly the rain tapers off 
this evening. models are aggressive in showing a dry slot developing 
which would help mix stronger winds aloft down to the surface. If 
precipitation lingers, it could stabilize the boundary layer 
and reduce mixing and therefor the maximum wind gusts.

KEY MESSAGE 3: The next chances of showers are expected to
return Sunday night into Monday as flow aloft remains fairly
zonal. This pattern supports fast moving systems typically of
lower amplitude. Model guidance remains fairly consolidated on a
quick-hitting system to move through mainly Sunday night and
early Monday. Expected 540dm isoheight placement on the 500mb
surface continues to be through northern New York and Vermont
keeping potential for mixed precipitation mainly at higher
elevations a possibility. However, daytime precipitation will
likely be lower elevation rain and higher elevation snow. Snow
levels are projected to drop Monday night, but the bulk of the
moisture should have exited by then. Given its fast progression
and transition appearance of warmer than freezing low-levels,
impacts are not expected to be significant. Beyond Monday, model
consensus points to warming temperatures as the 540dm line
shifts northward with the region remaining under mainly zonal
flow. Highs in the mid/upper 40s are probable by the second half
of the week.
